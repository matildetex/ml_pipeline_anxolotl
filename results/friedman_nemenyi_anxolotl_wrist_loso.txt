------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Note on statistical power at n=37
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Friedman test itself (omnibus, all models at once) has reasonable power at
n=37 subjects -- clearly better powered than the WESAD equivalent (n=15, see
friedman_nemenyi.py), which is why a global effect can be detected here even when
individual differences between models are modest. The Nemenyi post-hoc test is
the weaker link: it corrects for all pairwise comparisons among 7 models (21 pairs), which is conservative, and with
MacroF1 differences between the top models typically under 0.02-0.03, n=37 paired
subjects is still on the low side for detecting small-to-moderate pairwise effect
sizes after that correction. A significant Nemenyi pair here is a comparatively
strong claim; a non-significant pair does not prove the two models are equivalent,
only that this sample could not separate them at the corrected alpha.
